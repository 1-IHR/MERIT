MERIT · Clinical Annotation Study
Multi-Perspective Equitable Routing for ICU Triage

ICU triage requires clinicians to make rapid decisions about which patients need intensive care, often with incomplete information and under significant time pressure. The severity of a patient's acute presentation does not always capture the full picture. Their care history, their capacity to communicate, the appropriateness of intensive care given their illness burden, and whether structural factors are affecting how their needs are weighted can all bear on the quality of the triage decision.

ICU triage requires balancing two competing risks: undertriage, where a patient who needs intensive care does not receive it and may deteriorate irreversibly, and overtriage, where a patient is escalated unnecessarily, occupying a limited resource.

This study evaluates COUNSEL, a structured clinical decision support tool that reviews each case across three dimensions: acute clinical need, ethical proportionality of intensive care, and equitable access to triage. It aims to support the physician in making a more informed and equitable triage decision by surfacing clinical, ethical, and equity considerations that the presentation alone may not carry.

Your task

Thank you for taking part in this study. Each case is presented in two stages, and we ask that you complete them in order.

In Stage 1 you will be shown the clinical information available to the triage clinician at the time of the decision. Please review it carefully and answer the questions before moving on.

In Stage 2 you will be shown the COUNSEL summary for the same case, followed by a brief set of follow-up questions.

We ask that you complete all cases in one sitting.

There are no right or wrong answers. Your honest clinical experience is what this study requires.

All cases are drawn from de-identified patient records and contain no personally identifiable information.
